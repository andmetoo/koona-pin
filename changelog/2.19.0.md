- Collections can sit inside other collections, up to three levels deep. Drag one onto another to put it inside, or between two to place it there; a folded collection opens when you hold a dragged one over it. On the keyboard, Alt and the arrow keys move the focused collection up, down, into the one above or out a level.
- A collection shows its own bookmarks and those of the collections inside it, and its count adds them up. The collections inside appear as chips at the top of the list, and "Only this collection" narrows it to the collection's own bookmarks.
- In the sidebar, collections fold and unfold with the arrow next to them, and the app remembers which ones you folded.
- Each collection has a "…" menu: Rename, Icon and colour, New collection inside, Move to, and Delete. Collections can be renamed for the first time.
- Give a collection one of 40 icons or any emoji, and one of five colours. Without an icon it keeps its dot.
- Deleting a collection moves the collections inside it up a level, with everything in them. Its own bookmarks stay in the library without a collection, as before.
- "Move to" for bookmarks shows the collections as a tree. When you have many, type to find one, with the collection it sits in shown before its name.
- On a phone, a collection with collections inside shows a second row of chips under the first, and choosing a collection for a bookmark shows the tree, folded as you like.
- Importing bookmarks from Chrome, Edge or Firefox keeps your folders as collections inside collections, up to three levels. Before, only top-level folders became collections and the folders inside them became tags.
- Exports keep the tree: the JSON copy, the HTML file browsers import, and the CSV, which writes a collection as "Work › Docs". Restoring a copy brings the tree back, and sync carries every move to your other devices.
- A list shared from a collection includes the bookmarks of the collections inside it.
- A file dragged onto the window could replace the app with its raw text on a Mac. The app now takes the links, feed files and lists it can import, and ignores any other file you drop.
- Koona Pin lists (.kpin) have their own icon in the Finder on a Mac, in every view, instead of a preview of their text, and in File Explorer on Windows instead of the app's icon.
- A file dragged in after a card's picture was dragged is taken again: the list's pictures no longer start a drag of their own.
- A link or text dragged onto the search field goes into it.
- A new library starts with three links: how Koona Pin works, Firefox and Bun.
