- Reading looks more like a book: real italics instead of slanted letters, the serif drawn at the right optical size, and headings, spacing, captions and code that grow with the text size you pick.
- Long words break with hyphens in the article's language (feed posts use their feed's), lines end more evenly, links have a lighter underline, quotes are in italics, and section breaks are a short rule.
- Chinese, Japanese and Korean articles use fonts made for their own language, in both Serif and Sans, whatever language the app is in.
- Articles in Reading show their lead photo on top, as on the site. News sites like The Verge, TechCrunch, Wired and Ars Technica used to open with no picture at all.
- Pictures that load as you scroll, pictures in newer formats and pictures from sites still on plain http now show in Reading, feed posts and feed thumbnails. A picture that can't load is hidden instead of showing a broken icon.
- Settings are tidier: seven groups instead of nine sections. General stays open and holds the app's version and update check; the others fold to one line with a short summary, and Settings remember which ones you opened.
- The browser and AI assistants share one group, Connections, which opens by itself when saving from the browser stops working. Offline copies, the library folder, the search index and preview refresh are together under Storage.
